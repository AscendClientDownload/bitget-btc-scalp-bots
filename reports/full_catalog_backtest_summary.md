# Full 100-Strategy Catalog Backtest — Summary

Generated by `scripts/backtest_full_catalog.py` against a real year of
cached Bitget BTCUSDT 5-minute data (the same dataset bot01's backtests
use). Full per-strategy results: `reports/full_catalog_backtest.csv`.

Unlike the earlier 8-sample smoke test (which only proved the declarative
engine runs without crashing), this backtests all 100 strategies for real —
each strategy accumulated hundreds to thousands of trades over the year,
a statistically meaningful sample, unlike the 1-5 trades any individual bot
had accumulated live at the time this was run.

## Result

**0 of 100 strategies showed positive expectancy** with a meaningful trade
count (≥20 trades). Expectancy per trade clustered tightly in the -0.15% to
-0.25% range across nearly all 100, regardless of strategy family
(trend-following, mean-reversion, breakout, momentum, volume, confluence,
candlestick, statistical) — the same fee-floor finding as bot #1's earlier
15-variant search, now confirmed at 100x scale.

Best: `mr_006_bb_rsi_adx_filter` — 161 trades, 45.3% win rate, profit factor
0.527, still **-0.110% per trade / -6.53% over the year**.
Worst: `sz_007_spread_from_sma50` — -0.331% per trade.

## Decision (with the user)

There is no version of "improve these strategies" that means "make them
profitable" — the evidence at this point is strong and converging. What *is*
a real, data-driven action: **stop running the ~85 clearly-worse performers
live**, keeping only the top 15 by backtested expectancy (bot01 stays
separate as the original reference bot). This isn't a claim any of the 15
are profitable — they're the least bad, nothing more. See
`scripts/run_all_bots.py`'s `ACTIVE_STRATEGY_IDS` for the exact list and
`docs/RISK_DISCLAIMER.md` for why this doesn't turn into a profit claim.

Run `python scripts/run_all_bots.py --all` to run the full 100 again (e.g.
to regenerate this backtest after a code change).
